## 1. The attributability test (the main item)

From arXiv:2406.18489, with quotations, establish which of equations (3)–(6) express
forward-in-time causality and which backward, for each order. Do not
assume the correspondence from the numbering, write it out from the text.

Construct, in the same 16 coordinates `p(a,b,x,y)` and under the same uniformity condition:
- **F** — the convex hull of correlations causal forward only (for A ≼ B and
  B ≼ A, the conditions of the forward direction only);
- **B** — the same, backward only;
- **TS** — the main polytope of Stage B.

For each facet class of TS (including both new ones) compute the maximum of the
functional over F and over B in exact arithmetic. Classify:
- **mixed** — violated both on F and on B: the inequality holds only under the
  joint constraint, this is the answer to question 1 of the paper in its own sense;
- **forward- / backward-directed** — violated only on one of them;
- **general** — holds on both.

In addition: check whether TS coincides with F∩B (construct F∩B in H-form,
enumerate the vertices, compare the sets). If TS ⊊ F∩B, exhibit a vertex of
F∩B lying outside TS and the facet of TS that cuts it off. Check the point
`x = a, y = a⊕b` separately: does it lie in F, in B, in F∩B.

Calibration of the test: take GYNI (the forward reading) and check that on F it
holds while on B it can be violated. If the test says "general" about everything
indiscriminately — it is vacuous.
