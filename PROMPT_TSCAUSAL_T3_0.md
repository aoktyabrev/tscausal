# PROMPT TSCAUSAL stage T3.0 — three mirrors

The two-party branch is closed: lemma D6 (without selection every Mrini–Hardy process is a mixture
of one-way channels) and lemma D8 (reduction to a Bell scenario). The new question:
**does D6 generalize to three parties**, or does time symmetry without selection admit
indefiniteness of order starting from three parties.

The repository rules are unchanged: Rule 0, preregistration as a separate commit before the first
run, `RESULTS.md` generated by a script, every estimator calibrated on a case where it
can fail, violations count only after a recomputation in mpmath.

## Context from the architect's literature check (verify against the primary sources, with quotations)

- Baumeler, Costa, Ralph, Wolf, Zych, "Reversible time travel with freedom of choice",
  CQG 36, 224002 (2019), arXiv:1703.00779: any logically consistent deterministic
  classical dynamics is realized by reversible interactions; three parties can be mutually
  in each other's past and future.
- The Lugano process (Araújo–Feix, Baumeler–Wolf, NJP 18, 013036, 2016) violates causal
  inequalities; its map from outputs to inputs is irreversible (000 and 111 both give 000).
- arXiv:2201.11832: realization of all unitary extensions of three-party processes on
  time-delocalized subsystems, with a global past and future.
- Tobar–Costa, CQG 37, 205011 (2020), arXiv:2001.02511 — reversible dynamics with CTCs.

The main thing that has to be established from the sources: **in what state the global
past P is prepared in the reversible extension of Lugano** — fixed (in Mrini–Hardy's language this is
preselection) or arbitrary.

## T.0 — the structure of the terms (analytics + null space, gate)

For three parties A, B, C (qubits) write out, with quotations, the general form of the
Mrini–Hardy TS process matrix for N parties (if the paper gives only the two-party case — complete
it and mark it as an inference). In the regime without selection (u, v marginalized) find the null space
of the linear conditions in the Pauli basis:
- the dimension, the list of admissible types of terms;
- whether there are terms whose supports overlap (for example, A_O B_I and A_O C_I);
- whether there are tripartite terms (the input of one party from the outputs of two others).

Calibration: the same code on two parties reproduces the 19 basis elements (d = 2) from
Stage C.

The fork:
- **all admissible terms commute pairwise** → attempt to prove the generalization of D6
  (a mixture of one-way channels / causally separable processes) for three parties, and then
  for N; this closes the direction with a positive structural result;
- **there are non-commuting or tripartite terms** → the proof of D6 does not carry over,
  we go to T.1.

## T.1 — Lugano in the regime without selection

1. Construct the reversible extension of the Lugano process following the sources: the bijection
   (P, a_O, b_O, c_O) → (F, a_I, b_I, c_I).
2. Check three regimes: (a) P fixed, as in the source — calibration, the violation
   of the causal inequality from Baumeler–Wolf must be reproduced with their number;
   (b) P uniformly random, F discarded — this is the Mrini–Hardy regime without selection;
   (c) a search over all reversible extensions with minimal dimension of P, if there are finitely many.
3. For (b) and (c): membership in the class without selection (a check of the linear conditions) and
   the value of the causal inequality (three-party, from Baumeler–Wolf or Abbott et al.,
   Quantum 1, 39, 2017, with a quotation of the form and the bound).

The key question: does the violation survive averaging over a random P.

## T.2 — a search beyond Lugano (only if T.0 opened the door)

  forward formalism from the literature, with a quotation.
