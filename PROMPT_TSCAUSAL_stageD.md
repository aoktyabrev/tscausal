## The question of the stage

The classes cl3–cl6 (facets of the classical closure, not facets of TS) are violated
by quantum processes with a **definite** order: 3+√2 against the classical bound
4. The architect's hypothesis: the gap is created by the time symmetry of the operations
(a normalization constraint in both directions), and in a purely forward scenario there is none.
If so, this is the place where time symmetry gives rise to a quantum advantage absent
in the usual picture.

## D.1 — completeness of the classical closure (gate)

The classical bound 4 rests on a closure built from circuits of dimension
1–4. A proof is needed that larger dimensions add no vertices. Candidate
route: characterize classical TS operations (normalization forward and backward)
as bistochastic objects, decompose them into a mixture of extremal ones by an analogue
of Birkhoff's theorem, show that the extremal ones are realized in dimension ≤ 4.
If there is no proof — numerically: a search over classical circuits of dimension 5 and 6
gives no points outside the closure, and in the report the bound is marked as numerical, not
proved. A gap with an unproved classical bound is called exactly that in the conclusions.

## D.2 — control: the purely forward scenario (the decisive test of the interpretation)

The same scenario (no settings, p(a,b) = 1/4, definite order A→B), but
operations with forward normalization only (ordinary instruments), without the backward
constraint. Construct the classical polytope and the quantum maximum for
cl3–cl6 (or for their analogues in this scenario — if the functionals do not carry over
literally, state exactly how they were carried over).

- no gap → the Stage C gap is created by time symmetry, the hypothesis is alive;
- there is a gap → it is not about time, the architect's interpretation falls away, and this is
  recorded as a negative result.

Calibration: in the forward scenario, without a dimension restriction, a classical circuit
can transmit a through the channel. Check that this really saturates the
quantum maximum; if not — explain.

## D.3 — upper bound on the quantum value

See-saw gives only lower bounds. By lemma D6 one order suffices. Find the
upper bound on the maximum of cl3–cl6 over all quantum TS processes with a definite
order, without a dimension restriction. Approach options, at the executor's choice with a
justification:
- merge Alice's instrument and the channel into a single object and reduce the problem to
  prepare-and-measure with TS constraints, then an SDP hierarchy
  (NPA-like for prepare-and-measure);
- an analytical treatment, if the structure of the witness allows it.

Calibration of the hierarchy: on the classical bound and on a known
prepare-and-measure problem from the literature (with a quotation). If the upper bound coincides with
3+√2 to within tolerance — this is a Tsirelson-type bound for time-symmetric circuits.
If not — record the gap between the lower and the upper bound as a number.

## D.4 — dimension and real numbers

1. See-saw on qutrits and ququarts (d = 3, 4), at least 50 starts. Does the
   value grow beyond 3+√2?
2. The same see-saw restricted to real matrices (W̄ and the instruments real
   symmetric), d = 2, 3, 4. Is 3+√2 attained by real QM?
   If yes — the gap does not require i. If not — this is a direct link with the branch
   [[quantum-reconstruction]], and then it is necessary to check separately that the reality
   restriction did not break admissibility (control: real classical
   circuits still give the bound 4).

## D.5 — where the gap comes from (reconnaissance, not a gate)

Classical TS operations resemble bistochastic ones, whereas the quantum analogues are
not constrained by Birkhoff's theorem in the general case. But for qubits unital channels are
mixtures of unitary ones, and the gap is already there for qubits. Check: is the gap attained if
the instruments are restricted to mixtures of "unitarily rotated classical" operations?
Record the result as an observation, without conclusions about the mechanism, if the test is not
unambiguous.
